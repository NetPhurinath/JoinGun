# Chawin Thachai — Product Owner

## What I owned

I was responsible for gathering and organizing JoinGun's requirements, checking that the planned and implemented work matched those requirements, preparing related product documents, and making product decisions about what the team should build. I connected the user problem to the requirements, priorities, backlog, and core journey so the team could work toward a shared scope. The [requirements specification](../../01-requirements/01-spec/20260908-01-joingun.md), [product backlog](../../01-requirements/backlog.md), and [traceability matrix](../../01-requirements/traceability.md) record this product definition and its links to design, implementation, and tests. I also reviewed project materials and made decisions as new questions arose during development. Some gaps remain visible in the documents: joining and leaving still lack an approved, dedicated functional requirement and acceptance criteria, and the controlled before/after task-effort study is pending. I treat these as open product work rather than silently claiming full requirements coverage. My role was to help the team decide what to deliver and check that the result remained aligned with the intended user needs.

Reflection

1. Lessons Learned

As a Project Owner, I learned that AI can make software development faster and easier, especially when it comes to coding, designing, and solving problems. AI helped our team reduce development time and find solutions more quickly. However, I also learned that AI does not always understand the project requirements correctly. Sometimes, the generated code or suggestions did not match what we actually needed, so we had to review and test them carefully.

One thing that improved was our development speed because AI helped the team complete tasks faster. However, one thing that became more difficult was maintaining consistency between the requirements and the actual implementation. As a Project Owner, I had to communicate clearly with the team and make sure everyone understood the same goals.

2. My Position in the AI Era

I think I am still at the beginning of my career in the software industry. AI is becoming an important tool, and I believe software engineers need to learn how to work with it instead of depending on it completely. From this project, I realized that technical skills alone are not enough. Communication, problem-solving, teamwork, and understanding user needs are also important. As a Project Owner, I learned that making good decisions and understanding the project are things AI cannot fully replace.

3. My Future Plan

Next year, I plan to complete my internship and graduate from university. I want to gain real working experience and improve my technical and communication skills. I am also interested in learning more about software testing and business analysis because I enjoy understanding requirements and finding ways to improve software.

In five years, I hope to become an experienced IT professional who can work confidently with different teams, manage responsibilities, and use AI effectively to improve software quality and productivity.
